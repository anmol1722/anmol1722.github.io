01 / THE QUESTION

What makes a
meaningful competitor?

Similar prices do not always mean similar products. High star ratings do not explain what customers actually like. And thousands of reviews are only useful when you can find the signal.

This self-directed research project brings those problems into one workflow for a competitive analyst: find relevant alternatives, understand price positioning and trace customer feedback back to the original evidence.

01→05

Similarity needs context.

The matcher combines MiniLM text embeddings with CPU, RAM, storage, display, graphics and price features. Segment, price-band and variant guards remove plausible-looking comparisons that fail the business question.

A similarity score describes the matching logic. It does not establish that a customer would prefer one product over another.

Read the important limitations +

Prices are incomplete. Only 7,875 of 25,841 listings have a listed price (30.5%). Price comparisons describe that subset, with coverage varying by segment.

Sentiment is a comparative signal. Aspect detection uses cue patterns and can misread context. Automated positive shares are not absolute satisfaction scores.

The source is historical. There are no live prices, sales figures or price histories. Listing and review counts describe the dataset.

This is a research demonstration. No measured revenue lift, user study or business impact is claimed. The live app runs on a small free server with shared provider limits.
